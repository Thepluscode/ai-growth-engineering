# Growth Leak Teardown — totality services

Research date: 2026-08-19

## ICP fit
- **Observed:** London MSP providing managed IT, security, compliance and consultancy.
- **Inferred:** Strong fit.

## Current offer
- **Observed:** A site-wide free audit; on the security page, a network-security audit promises threat analysis, a report and recommendations.

## Primary CTA
- **Observed:** “Book your free audit.”

## Acquisition surfaces
- **Observed:** Service and sector pages, customer testimony, location pages, certifications, email, phone and audit forms.

## Observable funnel
- **Observed:** IT, security, consultancy or sector page → same free-audit CTA → booking/contact route.
- **Unknown:** Whether source intent changes audit scope, form completion, qualification and audit-to-opportunity rates.

## Proof
- **Observed:** 150+ organisations, named testimonials, ISO 27001/9001, stated retention and Feefo evidence.

## Likely commercial leak
- **Inference, not diagnosis:** One broad audit label serves several distinct buyer triggers, potentially weakening expectation-match and attribution for high-intent security visitors.

## Competitor patterns
1. **System Force IT:** spells out the 60-minute inputs and five-day written output ([source](https://systemforce.co.uk/free-it-audit/)).
2. **Central Technology:** gives security buyers a dedicated review process and residual-risk report ([source](https://www.ct.co.uk/services/cyber-security-review/)).
3. **BCN:** uses a named cyber assessment with defined scoring and recommendations ([source](https://bcn.co.uk/cyber-security/cyber-security-assessment/)).

## One hypothesis
Security-specific CTA copy using the existing audit delivery will increase sales-accepted security audits without creating a new offer.

## One proposed experiment
On security pages, test “Book your free Network Security Audit — get a threat report and recommendations” against “Book your free audit.” Retain source and compare accepted security conversations.
